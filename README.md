## Project Overview

Financial markets are rife with "common sense" advice and market folklore. Many investors rely on rules of thumb, anecdotes, or popular narratives that may not stand up to rigorous quantitative scrutiny. This project analyzes several widespread market myths, evaluates their empirical validity, and quantifies their potential impact on investor decision-making.

---

## Key Objectives

- **Identify** pervasive market myths and investor heuristics.
- **Analyze** these beliefs using robust financial data and statistical techniques.
- **Quantify** the impact of such myths on investment strategies and outcomes.
- **Educate** investors and financial enthusiasts on critical thinking and evidence-based investing.

---

## Methodology

1. **Literature Review**: Researched popular market myths and the psychology behind them.
2. **Data Collection**: Gathered historical market data from reputable financial sources.
3. **Hypothesis Testing**: Formulated and tested hypotheses related to each market myth.
4. **Statistical Analysis**: Used Python and relevant libraries for data analysis and visualization.
5. **Reporting**: Summarized findings, highlighting which myths are debunked or supported by data.

---

## Results & Insights

Key findings and visualizations can be found in the `results/` directory and in the project report notebook.  
Some preliminary insights include:

- Many widely held beliefs (e.g., "Sell in May and go away") have little or no statistical support.
- Cognitive biases, such as overconfidence and recency bias, drive many investment mistakes.
- Evidence-based strategies significantly outperform heuristic-driven ones over the long term.

_See the full report for detailed results and recommendations._
